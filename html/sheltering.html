Shelter Is Survival

At high altitude, a proper shelter can mean the difference between life and death. Whether it's a purpose-built hut, a four-season tent, or a snow cave dug in an emergency, knowing how to shelter is a core skill.

  • Tents for every terrain and season
  • Snow caves & bivouacs for emergencies
  • Alpine huts and base camp systems
  • Insulation, ventilation, and location choice

Understanding Each Shelter

Four-Season Tents

The most common mountaineering shelter. Built to withstand high winds and heavy snow loads, with reinforced poles and a sturdy fly.

  • Rated for 4 seasons (winter-capable)
  • Geodesic or tunnel designs for wind resistance
  • Examples: Hilleberg, MSR, Mountain Hardwear

Snow Caves

A life-saving shelter in emergencies. Snow provides excellent insulation — temperatures inside a well-built snow cave stay near freezing even when outside is -30°C.

  • Dig into a snow drift on a slope
  • Sleeping platform higher than entrance (cold air sinks)
  • Ventilation hole essential to prevent CO₂ buildup

Bivouac Sacks

Emergency or fast-and-light shelter — a waterproof outer layer around your sleeping bag. Used when you can't reach a tent or hut.

  • Ultra-lightweight and packable
  • Used in alpine-style fast ascents
  • Can be combined with a tarp or bothy bag

Alpine Huts

Fixed mountain shelters on popular routes in the European Alps. Staffed huts offer meals, bunks, and a warm place to recover.

  • Typically at 2,000–4,000 m elevation
  • Reservations often required in summer
  • Examples: Chamonix's Cosmiques, Zermatt's Hörnli

Base Camps

The logistical heart of an expedition. Base camps are semi-permanent villages with tents for sleeping, cooking, eating, and medical care.

  • Sites for weeks or months of operation
  • Larger teams, medical supplies, and communications
  • Examples: Everest BC, Denali BC

Portaledges

Hanging platforms for sleeping on big-wall climbs. Suspended from anchor points on vertical rock faces, portaledges allow multi-day ascents of El Capitan-sized walls.

  • Used in Yosemite, Patagonia, and Karakoram big walls
  • Suspended from bombproof anchors
  • Advanced skill: rigging and deployment
