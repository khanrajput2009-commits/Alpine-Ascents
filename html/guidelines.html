The Full Guidelines

01

Safety Is Non-Negotiable

The summit is optional — coming home is mandatory. Never compromise safety for summit fever. Turn around if conditions deteriorate, your team is exhausted, or you have any doubt.

02

Leave No Trace

Pack out everything you bring in — including waste. Bury human waste at least 30 cm deep, 60 m from water sources. Use established campsites. Don't disturb wildlife, plants, or historical artifacts.

03

Respect Local Cultures

In the Himalaya, Andes, and Africa, respect local customs, dress codes, and religious sites. Ask before photographing people. Support local businesses and hire local guides whenever possible.

04

Report Ascents Honestly

Report only ascents you actually completed. Note conditions, style (alpine vs. expedition), and any assistance used. Honesty protects the integrity of the sport and the safety of future climbers.

05

Prepare Thoroughly

Research your route, train physically and mentally, and know the weather forecast. Bring the proper gear — not too little, not too much. Have a clear emergency plan and share it with someone at home.

06

Support Your Team

Mountaineering is a team sport. Help others when you can, share resources, and never leave a partner behind. Follow the decisions of your designated leader. Respect porters, guides, and all support staff as equals.
