Our Legacy

A History Carved In Ice & Stone

From the first summit attempts to modern high-altitude ascents — the story of humanity's pursuit of the sky.

Since the 1700s
The Origins

The Birth of Mountaineering

Mountaineering as a sport was born in the European Alps. What began as scientific curiosity became a passionate pursuit — and eventually, an obsession that would take humanity to the roof of the world.

  • 1786 — Mont Blanc first climbed by Balmat & Paccard
  • 1854–1865 — The "Golden Age of Alpinism"
  • 1953 — Everest summit by Hillary & Tenzing
  • 1950s–60s — Himalayan era begins
Milestones

The Journey Through Time

Centuries of climbs, celebrations, and sacrifices that shaped mountaineering.

1786

First Ascent of Mont Blanc

Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard reach the summit of Mont Blanc (4,808 m), marking the birth of modern mountaineering.

1854–1865

The Golden Age of Alpinism

The rise of British mountaineers and Alpine Club saw the first ascents of most major Alpine peaks, including the Matterhorn (1865).

1865

The Matterhorn Conquered

Edward Whymper's party makes the first successful ascent, though the descent ends in tragedy — a turning point in climbing safety culture.

1922

Everest Attempts Begin

The first British expedition to Everest reaches 8,320 m — setting the stage for decades of Himalayan exploration.

1953

Everest Summited

Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reach the summit of Mount Everest — the defining moment of 20th-century mountaineering.

1978

First 8000m Without Oxygen

Reinhold Messner and Peter Habeler summit Everest without supplemental oxygen — redefining human limits.

1980

The Solo Everest Ascent

Reinhold Messner makes the first solo ascent of Everest without oxygen during monsoon season — one of history's greatest climbing achievements.

2000s–Today

The Modern Era

Commercial expeditions, precision gear, real-time weather data, and a new generation of climbers — safer, faster, and more ambitious than ever.
