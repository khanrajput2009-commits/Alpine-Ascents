Hazards & Safety

The mountains are unforgiving — but with knowledge, preparation, and respect, they become a place of wonder, not danger.

Understanding The Risks

Respect The Mountain, Return Home

Every mountaineer must understand the hazards that exist in the alpine environment. The most dangerous climber is the one who thinks they're safe — the best climber is the one who knows what they don't know.

  • Avalanche awareness & snow assessment
  • Altitude sickness prevention & treatment
  • Weather reading & storm avoidance
  • Rockfall, icefall, and serac awareness

Understanding Each Hazard

Avalanche

Avalanches kill more mountaineers than any other single hazard. Most victims trigger the slide themselves — understanding snow stability is critical.

  • Check avalanche forecast before every trip
  • Carry transceiver, probe, shovel — and know how to use them
  • Avoid slopes 30°–45° after recent snowfall

Altitude Sickness

Above 2,500 m, the body struggles to absorb oxygen. AMS, HACE, and HAPE can escalate from mild headache to fatal within hours.

  • Ascend slowly — no more than 500 m/day above 3,000 m
  • Descend immediately if symptoms worsen
  • Know the difference: AMS vs. HACE vs. HAPE

Rockfall & Icefall

Falling rock and ice are unpredictable and deadly. Rockfall is more common in the afternoon as the sun warms the mountain — climb early.

  • Wear a helmet — always
  • Move fast through exposed areas
  • Avoid climbing below seracs on warm days

Weather Storms

Mountain weather changes fast. Storms can move in within minutes — lightning, high winds, and whiteout conditions are all lethal.

  • Get a detailed forecast before every climb
  • Turn around if clouds build and thunder starts
  • Carry storm protection: shelter, layers, GPS

Crevasses

Hidden cracks in glaciers can be 30+ meters deep. Snow bridges over them may look solid but collapse without warning.

  • Rope up on any glacier — no exceptions
  • Probe suspicious snow bridges
  • Know crevasse rescue systems

Hypothermia

When the body loses heat faster than it can generate it, hypothermia sets in. It dulls judgment first — victims often don't realize they're in danger.

  • Stay dry — wet clothes drain body heat 25x faster
  • Eat high-calorie foods regularly
  • Watch for shivering, confusion, and slurred speech
