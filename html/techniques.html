How Each Technique Works

Rope Work & Knots

The foundation of all climbing safety. Every mountaineer must be able to tie essential knots blindfolded, in the dark, with gloves on.

  • Figure-eight follow-through (the "climbing knot")
  • Clove hitch, munter hitch, prusik
  • Alpine butterfly, double fisherman's

Belaying

The act of protecting your partner as they climb. Proper belay technique is what stands between a small slip and a fatal fall.

  • Belay devices: ATC, GriGri, Reverso
  • PBUS (pull, brake, under, slide) technique
  • Communicating with your climber

Anchor Building

A solid anchor is the bedrock of every safe pitch. Learn to build SERENE anchors and equalize multiple points of protection.

  • SERENE principles: Solid, Efficient, Redundant, Equalized, No Extension
  • Cordelette, quad, and sliding-X anchors
  • Natural vs. artificial pro (cams, nuts, pitons, ice screws)

Glacier Travel

Crossing a glacier safely requires roped travel, crevasse awareness, and the ability to perform a crevasse rescue.

  • Roped team travel with proper spacing
  • Crevasse rescue systems (Z-pulley, C-pulley)
  • Reading snow bridges and route hazards

Self-Arrest

If you fall on snow or ice, self-arrest is the technique that stops you from sliding to your death. It must be reflexive.

  • Ice axe grip: pick, adze, spike positions
  • Roll into the slope, drive pick into snow
  • Practice both head-up and head-down arrest

Navigation

When the weather turns and visibility drops, navigation skills are the difference between life and death on the mountain.

  • Map, compass, altimeter, and GPS
  • Terrain association and route-finding
  • Whiteout navigation and pacing
